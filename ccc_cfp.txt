Title: 

Abstract:

Have you ever pasted a bunch of blueprints in Factorio, only to
discover that the whole thing doesn't work, and it turns out
to be that one flipped inserter after a 3am coffee bender?
What if you could mathematically *guarantee* that your factory
does what you want it to do?
We present NAUVIS (Nearly AUtomated Verification of Industrial Systems),
a state-of-the-art formal verifier for factories.
Using NAUVIS, you can prove that your factories are belt safe, bug-free, 
and, most importantly, productive!
Along the way, we demonstrate the basic principles of formal
verification and how to design practical verifiers.

Description:

First, we figure out what it even means for factories to be "correct":
machines receive their expected inputs, the belt network is well-formed,
and the bugs stay outside.

Next, we describe how factories can be represented as network graphs
with constraints and correctness properties can be represented as
constraints over these graphs.

Then we show how to encode these graphs and their constraints as logical
formulas, which can be checked by automatic solvers.

The methodology underlying NAUVIS is widely used for designing formal
verification tooling, and many of the principles and limitations we
present are applicable to other domains, such as programming languages
and hardware specification.

THE FACTORY MUST (verifiably) GROW.
